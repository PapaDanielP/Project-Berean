# Allegheny County Pilot Source-Availability and Comparability Review

**Review status:** Complete — provisional determination

**Review date:** August 21, 2026

**Candidate county:** Allegheny County, Pennsylvania

**Corpus:** `pa-foster-care-placement-reunification`

**Corpus period:** January 1, 2018 through December 31, 2025

## 1. Formal determination

# PILOT_REVIEW_REQUIRED

Allegheny County has a credible public source base and appears suitable for a county-level research pilot, but the current evidence is not sufficient to authorize the pilot as selected. The strongest available sources establish county-specific relevance and several useful measures, but the review has not yet verified enough source-level denominator, cohort, definition, and reporting-period detail to support defensible statewide-versus-county numerical comparisons.

This is not a rejection of Allegheny County. It is a determination that the remaining source and comparability questions must be resolved before formal pilot authorization.

The charter remains provisional: one county pilot is still to be selected after source-availability review.

## 2. Governing selection criteria

The corpus charter requires the county pilot to have sufficiently complete, public, and stable documentation. The initial target is approximately 2–4 county-pilot documents. The corpus must preserve source identity, reporting period, population, denominator, methodology, definitions, qualifications, and provenance. Confidential case files, personally identifying information, individual child/family profiles, predictive risk scoring, and legal/social-work advice remain outside scope.

## 6. Subject coverage

The county source set provides useful coverage of:

- **Placement stability / placement dynamics:** placement types, placement duration, placement changes or moves as described by county materials, and dashboard placement dynamics.
- **Permanency:** permanency within 12 months for specified time-in-care cohorts, exits, adoption, permanent legal custodianship, and family-of-origin exits.
- **Reunification:** county service pages describe reunification as a permanency pathway; county narratives identify children returning to family and compare re-entry rates among exit groups.
- **Kinship care:** county sources identify kinship care as a preferred placement option and discuss first-placement type.
- **Congregate care:** county sources distinguish group homes and residential treatment settings.
- **Family preservation:** recent county budget narratives describe services during investigation and efforts to prevent unnecessary case opening or removal.
- **Re-entry:** county narrative reports re-entry within 12 months and distinguishes CYF/JPO involvement in the interpretation.
- **Visitation and family connections:** official county program pages and historical improvement materials discuss family visits, family search/engagement, and related practices, but the current review did not verify a 2018–2025 county metric series for visitation.

The charter does not require every listed topic to be represented. Education stability and sibling visitation remain decisions still requiring review under the charter.

## 10. Pilot sufficiency assessment

### Available strengths

- More than 2–4 attributable county-level documents or document surfaces are available.
- The sources cover placement dynamics, kinship, congregate care, permanency, reunification context, re-entry, and family-preservation services.
- The county and state source roles are distinguishable.
- The source set can be used at public aggregate, program, policy, dashboard, and performance-report levels.
- Recent county planning material provides coverage of portions of 2023–2025, while the dashboard landing page provides a 2010–2021 county time series description.

### Remaining deficiencies

- The current review does not establish a complete, consistently defined 2018–2025 county series.
- The dashboard requires a reproducible access and versioning protocol.
- Statewide and county metric denominators and cohort definitions have not yet been matched source-by-source.
- The current inventory does not yet contain the individual county documents as distinct rows with complete metadata.
- A direct, defensible comparison for placement stability specifically remains unresolved.
- The county material mixes fiscal-year and calendar-year reporting.

### Sufficiency conclusion

The source set is sufficient to justify continued review and a small manual source-record review. It is not yet sufficient to authorize Allegheny County as the selected pilot under the charter's comparability requirement.

## 11. Confidentiality and PII assessment

The strongest candidate sources are public aggregate, program, policy, performance, planning, or dashboard materials. They can be evaluated without requiring confidential case files, individual child records, individual family records, addresses, case identifiers, or names of children or families.

The public county pages also describe services and placement categories without requiring access to individual case records. Any future narrative or case-review source that contains unnecessary identifying details must be excluded or carefully redacted under the charter's privacy boundary.

No source requiring confidential information was used in this review.

## 12. Gaps and limitations

1. This review establishes source availability, not the truth of any county or statewide claim.
2. Search results and landing pages do not substitute for source-record verification.
3. The dashboard is dynamic and must be captured with access date, view, filters, and version/export metadata.
4. The current review does not verify every denominator, footnote, or table definition in the underlying reports.
5. Publication dates, fiscal years, calendar years, and dashboard observation periods must remain distinct.
6. A county measure with the same label as a statewide measure is not assumed comparable.
7. The source inventory contains candidate/state sources but does not yet provide a complete Allegheny-specific inventory.
8. The charter does not specify a minimum number of years or a required metric set for county selection beyond sufficient public, stable, comparable documentation.

## 14. Recommended next step

Resolve the comparability gaps before pilot authorization by performing a source-record review of the two strongest current county candidates:

1. The Allegheny County FY 2026–2027 Needs-Based Plan and Budget.
2. The Child Welfare Placement Dynamics dashboard and its reproducible view/export metadata.

For each, record exact citations, denominators, populations, definitions, period type, and qualifications. Then match one county measure to one Pennsylvania statewide measure. If at least one placement or permanency comparison is directly or conditionally comparable and the dashboard can be reproduced, update the source inventory and reconsider the formal pilot determination.

Until then, leave the charter's county selection provisional.
